# Home blood pressure is a sit, not a sprint from the car park

I used to treat a blood pressure number like weather. One ugly reading on a stool in a chemist, legs crossed, still warm from the walk, and I decided I was "a bit high when stressed." That number was a personality test. It was not a vascular one.

A clinic reading can lie in both directions. Some men only run high in a medical room. Others look fine in the surgery and sit high at 6am in their own kitchen. One kiosk printout is a coin flip. A week of dull, repeated sits is something a clinician can actually use.

I am not diagnosing hypertension. I am describing a method that stops you arguing with a single spike.

## Why the chemist machine is a poor ritual

Pharmacy cuffs live in fluorescent aisles. People lean forward. The cuff is the wrong size. Someone is waiting behind you. You just walked the shop. White-coat effect in clinic is real. Masked hypertension is the opposite problem and a one-off will not find it.

Guidelines keep repeating the same boring setup because the boring setup is the intervention. Home averages and ambulatory monitors beat "I felt fine at the dentist."

If a doctor already put you on a drug, this article does not get a vote. You measure how they told you to measure. You do not skip a tablet because a blog described a chair.

## The sit that makes the number mean something

Upper-arm cuff. Bare skin, not a hoodie. Back supported. Feet flat. Arm at heart level, not hanging and not shrugged. No talking. No scrolling. Five minutes of sitting is the part people skip because five minutes feels like a waste. It is the whole method.

Then two readings, about a minute apart. Log both. Morning, after the bathroom, before coffee, is a useful default. Evening is a second series if you are doing a proper week. Do not measure only after an argument. Do not measure only when you "feel high." That is how you confirm a mood.

Cuff size is not a vibe. Too small reads high. Too large reads low. Measure the mid-upper arm once, like an adult buying a shirt.

Wrist toys at the wrong height collect fiction. A validated upper-arm unit is the less theatrical choice.

## What the two numbers even are

Systolic is the top. Diastolic is the bottom. Units are millimetres of mercury because medicine is old. Home thresholds and clinic thresholds are not always identical, which is another reason a kiosk panic is not a care plan.

I will not paste a treatment table. Cutoffs move. Diabetes, kidneys, and a prior stroke change the conversation. A writer without your chart should not play internist. Repeated home averages in the range your clinician already called too high are a reason to use the phone, not a reason to buy a beetroot capsule.

Pulse pressure (the gap) is a cocktail fact. Do not build a religion on one gap from one evening.

## A week that was actually useful

After a clinic 148/92 while chatting about traffic, I ran six mornings at home with a five-minute sit and no talking. Most systolics sat in the high 120s to mid 130s. Diastolics lived in the low 80s. One 142 happened when I cheated: standing, radio on. I threw that row out of the average because it was a different experiment.

The useful output was a sheet. The clinician cared about the average and the method. They did not care about my theory of the kiosk.

Travel weeks look noisier. Hotel desks, espresso first, the wrong chair. That is information about habits, not a new disease.

If a home series suddenly sits in the 180s, or you have chest pain, breathlessness, a neurological change, or vision loss, you stop reading blogs. That is urgent care. A protocol is not an ambulance.

## How I write it down

Paper grid: date, time, arm, reading 1, reading 2, notes. The notes column is where the lies would go if I did not write them. "Talked through the cuff" is a ruined row. I want seven morning pairs before I bother anyone with a photo of a single 142.

Some apps average for you. Fine. I still want the raw pair. A device that only shows a wellness score is a toy.

Night-shift people cannot worship 6:40am. Measure after the longest sleep you actually get, before the stimulant, same ritual. Consistency beats cosmology.

If you already take a pressure drug, ask the prescriber when to measure relative to the pill. Skipping a dose to "see the real number" is usually a bad experiment.

Sharing a cuff with a partner is fine only if the cuff still fits both arms. It often does not.

## Failure modes I have personally used

Talking. Caffeine. Full bladder. Legs crossed. Measuring over clothing. One reading, then a story. Competing with yesterday's number in real time, which raises the next one.

Some machines flag arrhythmia. If the device keeps complaining, that is a clinician question, not a battery question.

People with certain arm issues, fistulas, or lymph node dissections get a specific method from the person who operates on them. Do not crowd-source which arm.

I keep that page next to the cuff because I still try to rush the sit when I am late.

## What this is not

It is not vascular age. It is not a methylation test. It is a pressure in a tube, averaged, with a method. Breathwork to "lower it for the reading" measures breathwork. If you want the number your vessels live with, sit quietly without performing.

Salt can move pressure in salt-sensitive people. That is a dietetics conversation, not a weekend challenge I will prescribe from here. Training raises pressure. Measure on a rest morning if you are hunting a baseline.

A week of sits will not make you interesting at dinner. It will make the next clinic visit shorter. That is the whole prize.
