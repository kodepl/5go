The human brain is one of the most complex and fascinating organs in the known universe. Despite decades of intensive research, scientists are still uncovering mind-blowing facts about how our brains work, process information, and shape who we are.

## Your Brain Contains 86 Billion Neurons

The average adult brain contains approximately **86 billion neurons**, each connected to thousands of other neurons through synapses. This creates a neural network of unprecedented complexity, with more possible connections than there are stars in the Milky Way galaxy. Every thought, memory, and emotion you experience emerges from the intricate dance of electrical and chemical signals across this vast network.

## The Brain Uses 20% of Your Energy

Although your brain represents only about **2% of your body weight**, it consumes roughly **20% of your daily energy intake**. This disproportionate energy usage reflects the constant activity required to maintain consciousness, process sensory information, regulate bodily functions, and support cognitive processes. Your brain is literally the hungriest organ in your body.

## You Generate New Brain Cells Throughout Life

For many years, scientists believed that humans were born with all the brain cells they would ever have. However, modern research has revealed that **neurogenesis**—the creation of new neurons—continues throughout adulthood, particularly in the hippocampus, a region crucial for learning and memory. This discovery has profound implications for understanding brain plasticity and recovery from injury.

## Your Brain Never Stops Working

Even when you're asleep, your brain remains incredibly active. During sleep, your brain processes information from the day, consolidates memories, clears out toxins, and repairs itself. The idea that we only use 10% of our brains is a myth—in reality, we use virtually all of our brain, just not all at once.

## Brain Training Can Actually Work

Engaging in mentally stimulating activities can strengthen neural connections and even create new ones. Learning new skills, solving puzzles, reading challenging material, and engaging in meaningful conversations all contribute to **cognitive reserve**—the brain's resilience against age-related decline.

Understanding these fascinating facts about your brain can help you appreciate its complexity and take better care of this remarkable organ that makes you who you are.
